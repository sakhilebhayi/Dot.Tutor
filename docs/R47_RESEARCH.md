### 1. Children's Act 38 of 2005 and the two registers

- **Definition of a child.** A child is "a person under the age of 18 years" (s1), and a child becomes a major at 18 (s17). Verified: https://source.acts.co.za/childrens-act-2005/1_interpretation.php, https://www.acts.co.za/childrens-act-2005/17_age_of_majority
- **Who goes into Part B.** A children's court, any other court, or a disciplinary forum may find a person unsuitable to work with children. Conviction of listed offences (murder, rape, sexual offences against a child, child pornography, certain trafficking offences) makes a person unsuitable automatically (s120). Verified: https://www.acts.co.za/childrens-act-2005/120_finding_persons_unsuitable_to_work_with_children
- **Effect of a listing.** A listed person may not manage or operate, or work with or have access to children at, a child and youth care centre, a school, a "club or association" or similar institutions, or "other employment or activities" that may be prescribed. Managers and operators of those institutions may not let a listed person work with children (s123). Verified: https://www.acts.co.za/childrens-act-2005/123_consequences_of_entry_of_name_in_part_b_of_register
- **The duty to check (s126).** Before a person works with or has access to children, the following must establish whether that person is in Part B: institutions providing welfare services to children (child and youth care centres, partial care facilities, shelters, drop-in centres, schools), designated child protection organisations, SAPS child protection units, state departments and municipal councils. Existing staff had to be checked within 12 months of commencement. Inquiries go in writing to the Director-General, who must answer within 21 working days. Verified: https://www.acts.co.za/childrens-act-2005/126_establishment_of_information_in_part_b_of_register
- **Do private online platforms or independent tutors fall within s126? UNCERTAIN.** The s126 list names institutions and public bodies. It does not clearly cover a private online marketplace or a self-employed tutor. Section 123's "club or association" and "prescribed" activities could widen the reach, but the wording read does not settle the question. A 2019 Department of Social Development statement says "all organisations working with, or having access to, children" must verify suitability. That is a policy statement, not the statutory text. Verified: https://www.gov.za/news/media-statements/social-development-calls-employers-verify-their-employees-child-protection
- **How a check is requested. Correction to the brief:** the employer uses **Form 29**, not Form 30. Under regulation 44, Form 29 goes to the Director-General in a sealed envelope marked confidential, on letterhead, signed, with a certified copy of the signatory's ID. Verified: https://www.acts.co.za/childrens-act-2005/r261_44__establishment_of_information_in_part_b_of_national_child_protection_register. Form 30 is a person's inquiry about their own name. Seen only through a Western Cape form listing, so not verified as primary text: https://wcedonline.westerncape.gov.za/branchIDC/ECD/ECD-PartialCare/Form%2030.pdf. The Department of Justice says Form 29 is emailed to CPRenquiries@dsd.gov.za. Verified: https://www.justice.gov.za/vg/nrso.html
- **National Register for Sex Offenders (Act 32 of 2007, Chapter 6).** The Department of Justice says employers must vet people placed in charge of children or persons with mental disabilities. It defines an employer as any public or private organisation, club, person or association. Applications use Form 8 or Form 8A (J958), can be made at any provincial office, cost nothing, and can be emailed to nrso@justice.gov.za. A listed employee must be moved to a post without access to vulnerable persons, or their contract terminated. Verified: https://www.justice.gov.za/vg/nrso.html. The statutory text of s45 (the employer's obligation) could not be read because acts.co.za returned 404. Not verified.
- **No public API.** The Department of Justice says the NRSO "is not open to the public and is kept confidential", and that page mentions no online lookup. Verified (same URL). For both registers, the procedures found are paper or email inquiries with a 21-working-day statutory response time for Part B. No API or online lookup was found for either register (Not verified as an absolute; absence of evidence only).

### 2. POPIA (Act 4 of 2013)

- **s1 definitions.** A "child" is a natural person under 18 "who is not legally competent, without the assistance of a competent person, to take any action or decision" about themselves. A "competent person" is "any person who is legally competent to consent to any action or decision being taken in respect of any matter concerning a child". Verified (statute reproduced): https://popia.co.za/section-1-definitions/
- **s34.** "A responsible party may, subject to section 35, not process personal information concerning a child." Verified: https://popia.co.za/section-34-prohibition-on-processing-personal-information-of-children/
- **s35(1) exceptions.** The prohibition does not apply to processing (a) with the prior consent of a competent person; (b) necessary to establish, exercise or defend a right or obligation in law; (c) necessary to comply with international public law; (d) for historical, statistical or research purposes, with safeguards; (e) of information the child deliberately made public with a competent person's consent. Verified: https://popia.co.za/section-35-general-authorisation-concerning-personal-information-of-children/
- **s35(2)-(3) authorisation.** The Regulator may authorise processing by Gazette notice where it is in the public interest and safeguards are in place. Possible conditions include parental review and refusal rights and not encouraging a child to disclose more than is reasonably necessary. Verified (same URL).
- **Regulator guidance.** The Information Regulator's Guidance Note on processing personal information of children (June 2021, 14 pages) covers only the s35(2) authorisation route. Applications go to authorisationIR.PIC@justice.gov.za. The note calls itself guidance only. It says nothing specific about online services, apps or education. Verified (text extracted from PDF): https://inforegulator.org.za/wp-content/uploads/2020/07/GuidanceNote-Processing-PersonalInformation-Children-20210628-1.pdf. No Regulator guidance on online services for children was found.

### 3. Films and Publications Act 65 of 1996 (amended by Act 11 of 2019)

- **Commencement.** The 2019 amendments took effect on 1 March 2022. Commercial online distributors must register and classify content. Members of the public posting user-generated content who are not commercial distributors do not need to register. The FPB handles complaints about non-commercial distributors. Verified (government news agency): https://www.sanews.gov.za/node/56637
- **s24C, child-oriented services.** This applies to anyone providing "child-oriented services, including chatrooms". A contact service is one that lets previously unacquainted people make contact. Operators must moderate, display child-friendly safety messages, give children a way to report suspicious behaviour, report suspected offences against children to SAPS and, where feasible, tell parents about filtering tools. The penalty is up to R50,000 and/or 6 months. Verified: https://www.acts.co.za/films/24c__obligations_of_internet_a. Whether a tutoring platform connecting adult tutors with children is a "child-oriented contact service" is UNCERTAIN.
- **s27A, internet service providers.** ISPs must register with the Board, take reasonable steps to prevent hosting of child pornography and, once aware of it, block access, report it to SAPS, preserve evidence and give user particulars to SAPS on request. Penalties run to R750,000 and/or 5 years. The text read uses "child pornography" and contains no express take-down duty. Verified: https://www.acts.co.za/films/27a__registration_and_other_obligations_of_internet_service_providers. Whether that consolidation reflects the later change in terminology to "child sexual abuse material" is not verified. Whether a platform operator counts as an "ISP" under s27A is UNCERTAIN.
- **Reporting and complaints channels.** Complaints form: https://complaints.fpb.org.za/. CSAM hotline: https://apps.fpb.org.za/hotline/ (0800 424 247). Distributor self-registration: https://fpb.org.za/distributor-registration/. Verified: https://www.fpb.org.za/
- **Do user-generated content platforms have to register? UNCERTAIN.** The FPB's 2022 notice under ss24C and 27A required covered providers to report within 90 days how they comply. It was found only through secondary reporting. Not verified: https://www.gov.za/sites/default/files/gcis_document/202210/47373gon2682.pdf

### 4. Consumer Protection Act 68 of 2008 and ECT Act 25 of 2002

- **CPA s16.** The cooling-off right applies only to transactions resulting from direct marketing: 5 business days after the later of conclusion of the agreement or delivery, with a refund within 15 business days. Under s16(1) it "does not apply to a transaction if section 44 of the Electronic Communications and Transactions Act applies". Verified: https://www.acts.co.za/consumer-protection/16_consumer_s_right_to_cooling_off_period_after_direct_marketing
- **CPA s14, fixed-term agreements.** A consumer may cancel at any time on 20 business days' written or recorded notice, subject to a reasonable cancellation penalty. The supplier must give notice 40 to 80 business days before expiry. Agreements continue month to month unless the consumer chooses otherwise. Transactions between juristic persons are excluded. Verified: https://www.acts.co.za/consumer-protection/14_expiry_and_renewal_of_fixed_term_agreements. The commonly cited 24-month maximum comes from the regulations and is not verified.
- **CPA s39, minors.** An agreement with an unemancipated minor made without the consent of an "adult responsible for that minor" is voidable at the consumer's option unless it is later ratified. This does not apply if the minor induced the supplier to believe they had full capacity. Verified: https://source.acts.co.za/consumer-protection-act-2008/39_agreements_with_persons_lacking_legal_capacity.php
- **ECT s43.** Online suppliers must publish 18 listed items, including identity and addresses, full price, terms, refund policy, privacy and security policies and s44 rights. Consumers must be able to review and correct an order before placing it. If the supplier fails, the consumer may cancel within 14 days. Verified: https://source.acts.co.za/electronic-communications-and-transactions-act-2002/43__information_to_be_provided.php
- **ECT s44.** For services, a consumer may cancel without reason or penalty within 7 days after the agreement is concluded. The only charge allowed is the direct cost of returning goods, and refunds are due within 30 days. Verified: https://source.acts.co.za/electronic-communications-and-transactions-act-2002/44__cooling_off_period.php
- **ECT s42(2) exclusions.** Section 44 does not apply to services "which began with the consumer's consent before the end of the seven-day period" (s42(2)(d)), among others. Verified: https://source.acts.co.za/electronic-communications-and-transactions-act-2002/42__scope_of_application.php
- **How the two Acts interact.** For lesson packages sold online, ECT s44 appears to govern and to displace CPA s16. How s42(2)(d) applies to a package where the first lesson falls inside the 7 days is UNCERTAIN.

### Questions for a South African lawyer

1. Is a marketplace that connects parents with independent tutors one of the bodies s126 of the Children's Act obliges to check Part B, or caught by s123's "club or association" or prescribed activities? And can such a platform lawfully submit Form 29 for tutors who are not its employees?
2. Is the platform an "employer" under s45 of the Sexual Offences Act for independent contractor tutors? Does the Department of Justice's broad definition ("any public or private organisation ... person") reflect the statute?
3. Under POPIA, may a learner give consent alone at any age below 18, or must a parent or guardian, as "competent person", consent for every learner from Grade 1 to Grade 12?
4. Does an online tutoring service for children need s35(2) authorisation from the Information Regulator, or is competent-person consent under s35(1)(a) enough?
5. What consents, notices, retention limits and access controls are needed to record video lessons with minors? Can a parent opt a child out while the platform keeps safeguarding recordings?
6. Is a tutoring platform a "child-oriented contact service" under s24C of the Films and Publications Act, with its moderation, reporting and safety-message duties? Does s27A (as an ISP) or FPB registration apply to it?
7. What must the platform do on discovering suspected abuse or CSAM? Report to SAPS, the FPB hotline or social services? Does s110 of the Children's Act (mandatory reporting) apply to platform staff or tutors?
8. For prepaid lesson packages sold online, does ECT s44 or CPA s16 apply? Does s42(2)(d) remove the 7-day cooling-off once the first lesson starts? How should refunds of unused lessons be handled?
9. Is a monthly lesson subscription a fixed-term agreement under CPA s14? What cancellation penalty would count as "reasonable"?
10. If a minor buys lessons without parental consent, what follows from CPA s39 and the common law on minors' contracts, and how should checkout verify an adult payer?
11. Must tutors be registered with SACE to tutor school learners online for pay, or does that apply only to educators employed in schools?
12. How should the platform handle tutors outside South Africa, who cannot be checked against either South African register?

### What the safety domain did with this

- Everyone under 18 is treated as a child for consent (POPIA s1, Children's Act s1); the child/teen split only changes what a guardian approves.
- Guardian consent is recorded as competent-person consent under POPIA s35(1)(a), with version and text hash; whether s35(2) authorisation is also needed is question 4 above.
- Screening is recorded by operators against both registers (Form 29, Form 8) with evidence; it is not automated and not claimed as a legal obligation met (questions 1, 2 and 12).
- Moderation, safety notes on minor conversations and a child-facing report path exist; whether that meets FPB s24C is question 6.
- Minors' purchases wait for a guardian (CPA s39); cooling-off rules for lesson packages are left to the money domain and question 8.

## Courses, libraries and assessment (learning agent, 2026-11-10)

### Course structure
- **Udemy:** a course is sections of lectures (video, article, quiz, coding exercise, assignment) plus downloadable resources per lecture; a course page shows objectives ("What you'll learn"), requirements, total hours, last-updated date and language. Instructors set a price inside platform tiers and Udemy runs frequent site-wide discounts (unverified). Dot.Tutor takes the structure (sections, lessons, materials, objectives, requirements, workload) but not the perpetual discounting.
- **Coursera:** university-backed courses grouped into modules with graded assignments, peer review and deadlines; certificates verified with a shareable URL (unverified detail). Taken: completion rules and a public verification page, without implying accreditation.
- **Skillshare:** short project-based classes inside one subscription; creators are paid from a royalty pool by minutes watched (unverified). Not taken: Dot.Tutor sells per course so tutors control price, which suits school curricula.
- **Khan Academy:** free, mastery-based: skills levelled by practice (attempted, familiar, proficient, mastered) and course challenges; no certificates (unverified detail). Taken: topic mastery computed from answers, levels only after enough evidence.
- **Brilliant:** interactive problem-first lessons, subscription (unverified). Taken: practice activities as a lesson type and immediate marked feedback.

### Pricing and refunds
- Udemy offers a 30-day refund window with abuse limits (unverified). Coursera subscriptions offer a short refund period (unverified). Learners and parents expect a written refund rule before paying; Dot.Tutor therefore refuses to publish a paid course without a refund policy and shows it beside the price. The South African Consumer Protection Act and ECT Act cooling-off rules for online sales need a legal review before the money domain fixes defaults (owner item, unverified reading).

### Resource licensing and the past-paper problem
- **Quizlet** and **Chegg** host large volumes of user-uploaded material; both have faced complaints and court cases about copyrighted questions and answer keys (unverified detail). Lesson: provenance and licence must be captured at upload, and a takedown path must exist from day one.
- South Africa's Department of Basic Education publishes NSC past papers and memoranda for free download on education.gov.za (unverified at time of writing; confirm the page before relying on it). IEB papers are sold by the IEB and are not free to redistribute (unverified). Rule built: past papers only as "official source" with the publisher's link, held for a reviewer to confirm.
- ECT Act 25 of 2002, section 77, sets out take-down notifications for service providers (desk reading, not legal advice). Built: a complete notice hides the file pending an operator, the owner can counter, upheld files cannot be re-uploaded by hash.
- Creative Commons BY and BY-SA allow redistribution with attribution (BY-SA requires share-alike); "all rights reserved" third-party material cannot be shared.

### The cheating problem
- Contract-cheating services and answer-sharing sites are the main integrity risk named by universities (unverified). Platform measures within reach: never send answer keys before submission, server-side marking and timing, shuffled order, attempt limits, reveal-after-submit as a tutor choice, and treating practice separately from assessed results. AI-assisted cheating is out of scope for this domain (AI agent, wave 2).

### Progress design
- Leaderboards and streak pressure push surface engagement over learning; the brief forbids leaderboards. Khan-style mastery and spaced repetition (SM-2, as used by SuperMemo and Anki, unverified attribution of current Anki defaults) show improvement in plain terms. Built: per-topic recent accuracy and trend with thresholds in config, fixed-template sentences, and an explicit label that the text is computed, not AI and not an exam result.

### What Dot.Tutor does differently (built in this branch)

- **Explained, structured matching** instead of a popularity directory or a black-box agent: every result lists its scoring terms and plain reasons, and there is no paid placement (docs/R47_MATCHING.md).
- **Curriculum and level are data** (CAPS, IEB, Cambridge, tertiary) and part of the fit, through the academic seam, rather than a free-text "subject".
- **Teaching language is a matching dimension** (isiZulu, Sesotho, Afrikaans ... and bilingual tutoring), never inferred from the interface language.
- **Verification levels from evidence** with an operator review and an audit trail, and labels that cannot claim more than was checked (a peer tutor is never shown as a qualified teacher).
- **Fair to newcomers**: a capped new-tutor term and a minimum review count, so a tutor is discoverable before they have reviews but cannot outrank a strong record on an equal fit.
- **Low commitment and many formats**: short consultations, homework help, exam prep, revision, groups, packages and weekly lessons, each with its price and cancellation terms shown before booking.
- **A tutor's own storefront and offerings**, so a student tutor can grow into an independent tutor and a small business on the same profile (brief section 18).
- **Minors**: bookings by minors wait for a guardian through the safety seam; the marketplace never auto-approves.
