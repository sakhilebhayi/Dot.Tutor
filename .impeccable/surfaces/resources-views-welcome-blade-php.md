Scope: public landing (resources/views/welcome.blade.php), Persuade mode. Audience: South African learners from Grade 1 to doctoral level, their guardians, and people who want to tutor. Job: find a tutor (sign in with InfoDot), second job: apply to teach. Proof on hand: the academic structure (CAPS phases, tertiary stages), the eleven official languages plus three, real open-licence photographs of South African libraries. Absent and never invented: tutors, counts, reviews, logos, earnings.

## Direction contract

THESIS: Dot.Tutor is a lending library of people: every level has its shelf, every tutor a catalogue record that says what they teach, at which levels, in which languages, and what was verified. It refuses the marketplace grid of smiling tutor cards, star ratings and a search-bar hero.

OWN-WORLD: shelf-label white ground tinted toward reading-room green; deep green-black ink; one reading-room green for actions; eight binding colours, one per academic stage (marigold, leaf, teal, cobalt, oxblood, plum, deep green, slate) used as data, never decoration. Archivo condensed for headings, Atkinson Hyperlegible Next for reading. Square panels that share edges, 4px corners on labels and controls, no shadows at rest, no gradients.

STORY: the visitor learns that they can say what they need in plain words, that Dot.Tutor matches by subject, level and language and shows why, that every level has its own room, and that tutors can build a practice. They sign in to find a tutor or apply to teach.
